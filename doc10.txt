Blockchain is a distributed digital ledger technology that is used to record and store transactions in a secure and transparent manner. 
In a blockchain network, information is organized into groups called blocks, and each block is connected to the previous block using cryptographic techniques. 
This structure makes it difficult to change previously recorded information without being detected. Unlike a traditional database controlled by a single organization, 
a blockchain can be maintained by multiple participants in a distributed network. Consensus mechanisms are used to help participants agree about the validity of transactions. 
Blockchain technology is widely associated with cryptocurrencies, but it can also be used in supply chain management, digital identity, healthcare records, secure document 
verification, and financial services. Smart contracts are programs that can automatically execute predefined rules on a blockchain. Important advantages of blockchain include transparency, 
traceability, and resistance to unauthorized modification. However, scalability, privacy, energy consumption, and regulatory issues remain important challenges.
